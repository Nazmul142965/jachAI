JachAI · signed-in UI

Three design directions, and one recommendation

Built on an inventory of the existing UI: 5 workspace roles, 15 screens, 22 views, 116 API paths. The palette below is the product's own theme.css, so these are layout proposals, not a repaint.

What the research says

Three product categories matter here, because JachAI is all three at once.

CategoryWhat mature products doWhat it means for JachAI
Multi-tenant SaaS
Linear, Notion, Vercel, Figma		 The workspace switcher is the highest-leverage control in the product: it decides which reality every other screen renders. It sits top-left in the sidebar or app chrome, carries a name and a distinct avatar, and lists each workspace with the viewer's role on the row. The personal account menu is a separate home, bottom-left or top-right, so switching tenant and managing yourself are different levers. This is JachAI's central problem, not a detail. One account can be a learner in one workspace and own another, and personal practice sits outside every workspace. The split has to be visible at all times.
Assessment & hiring
HackerRank, Codility, TestGorilla		 Auto-graded runs against real cases, and candidate comparison is a first-class screen rather than a report. Integrity tooling is prominent but framed as signals. Codility's differentiator is explicitly methodology and compliance. JachAI already has comparison, integrity signals and an audit trail. They should be presented as the serious core of the Talent product, not buried under a campaign list.
Consumer practice
Duolingo, Khan Academy, Brilliant		 Streaks, daily targets, progress meters and celebratory feedback, grounded in behavioural psychology; a 7-day streak measurably lifts retention. Adopt the structure, not the confetti. A daily target and a visible quota fit JachAI's AI practice quota exactly. Badges and celebration animations would undercut the credibility of a product whose other half issues verifiable certificates to employers.

The three directions

Direction A

Two scopes, one shell

Persistent sidebar. Workspace switcher pinned top, account menu pinned bottom. Role decides the home and which groups appear.

  • Conventional, so nothing has to be learned
  • Keeps the account/workspace split permanently visible
  • Weak during an attempt: the chrome is a distraction and an exit route
Direction B

Task-first focus mode

No persistent nav. Home is a ranked queue of what to do next; opening work fills the screen. Navigation by command palette and breadcrumbs.

  • Excellent for the attempt and for grading runs
  • Makes "what should I do now" the product's first answer
  • Discovery suffers badly: 15 screens with no visible map
Direction C

Product switcher console

Academy, Talent and Practice become three top-level products behind a switcher, each with its own nav. Workspace is implied by the product.

  • Reads as a large, serious platform
  • Scales if more products are added later
  • Wrong boundary: the real scope is the workspace, not the product — a learner in one org and an owner of another would be switching twice

Recommendation — Direction A as the shell, with B's focus mode for exactly two screens.

A is right for the 13 screens where people navigate, because the account/workspace split is JachAI's defining structure and the research is unambiguous that it should be permanently visible. B is right for the two screens where people work — taking an attempt, and grading one — because chrome there is a distraction and an accidental exit. C is rejected: it switches on the product when the real scope boundary is the workspace, so a person holding roles in two organizations would switch twice to get anywhere.
