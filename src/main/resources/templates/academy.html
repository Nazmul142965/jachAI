How an Academy cohort actually runs

Seven steps from workspace to verified certificate.

  1. 1

    Create the workspace and invite people

    An organisation admin adds instructors and learners by email invitation. Roles decide what each person can see; the server re-checks membership on every request.

  2. 2

    Build a question bank

    Authors write single-choice, multiple-choice or manually graded short-answer questions. A draft only becomes usable once a human approves it — generated drafts included.

  3. 3

    Publish a versioned assessment

    Publishing freezes the question set, time limit, pass mark and the result-release policy for that version, so later edits cannot change a result that already exists.

  4. 4

    Group learners into courses and batches

    A dated batch distributes a published version to every enrolled learner, and new enrolments receive it automatically. Progress and cohort analytics stay tenant-scoped.

  5. 5

    Learners take a server-timed attempt

    The deadline comes from the server, not the browser. Answers save individually with visible state, an attempt can be resumed, and submission is confirmed before it is final.

  6. 6

    Grade, release, and give feedback

    Short answers are graded by staff, then results are released either on submission or deliberately later. Instructors can publish feedback that only the owning learner can read.

  7. 7

    Issue certificates that verify publicly

    A passing, released attempt can receive one certificate. Anyone can check its code on a public page, which shows the organisation and assessment names — never the learner's private identity.

Deterministic scoring

Objective answers are scored by the server at submission; AI never sets or changes an official score.

Honest states

Pending manual grading, unreleased results and unavailable integrations are shown as they are — no invented progress.

Certificates with a public check

Every issued certificate has a verification code and a public page that reports valid or revoked, and when.

Plans and limits

  • The FREE plan allows 3 published assessments, 30 email invitations and 30 certificates per month.
  • Limits are enforced by the server before the write, not by the browser.
  • Paid plans are activated only after server-side payment verification.
  • Compare FREE and TEAM →

What Academy does not claim

  • Payments work end to end in demo mode: you pay, your plan changes and you get a receipt by email, but no real money is taken.
  • AI drafting is optional, needs a configured provider, and always produces drafts for human review.
  • Email delivery depends on a configured mail transport; the interface never claims a message was delivered.
